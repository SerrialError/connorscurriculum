## Formulas

| Formula | Symbols |
|---|---|
| $c = \lambda\nu$ | $c$ = speed of light ($2.998 \times 10^{8}$ m/s), $\lambda$ = wavelength (m), $\nu$ = frequency ($\text{s}^{-1}$) |
| $E = h\nu$ | $h$ = Planck's constant ($6.626 \times 10^{-34}$ J·s), $E$ = energy (per photon) |
| $\Delta E = E_f - E_i$ | change in energy between two levels |
| $\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = R_H\left(\dfrac{1}{n_f^2} - \dfrac{1}{n_i^2}\right)$ | $R_H = 1.096776 \times 10^{7}\ \text{m}^{-1}$ (Rydberg constant) |
| $E = (-hcR_H)\left(\dfrac{1}{n^2}\right) = (-2.18 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{J})\left(\dfrac{1}{n^2}\right)$ | energy of level $n$ |

Jaya's note: "$-2.18 \times 10^{-18}$ J = light emission."

<Callout type="warning">
**Corrections:**

- With $n_f$ first, $\frac{1}{\lambda} = R_H\left(\frac{1}{n_f^2} - \frac{1}{n_i^2}\right)$ comes out
  negative for emission, and a wavelength can't be negative. Write the **lower** level first:
  $\frac{1}{\lambda} = R_H\left(\frac{1}{n_{\text{low}}^2} - \frac{1}{n_{\text{high}}^2}\right)$.
- A **negative $\Delta E$** means light is emitted. $E_n$ itself is always negative because the
  electron is bound, whether or not anything is emitted. (For a single level the teacher grades only
  the magnitude; the sign of $\Delta E$ is what counts.)
- Her $hcR_H$ step checks out: $(6.626 \times 10^{-34})(2.998 \times 10^{8})(1.096776 \times 10^{7}) = 2.18 \times 10^{-18}$ J.
</Callout>

## Quantum numbers

- **$n$** = shell, the principal quantum number. It can be essentially any value (up to 6 on the Bohr model).
- **$l$** = subshell, the angular momentum quantum number (s, p, d, f), which gives the shape. Values from $n - 1$ down to 0.
- **$m_l$** = $-l$ to $+l$, the magnetic quantum number.
- **$m_s$** = $+\tfrac{1}{2}$ or $-\tfrac{1}{2}$, the spin. It can be ↑ or ↓.

<Callout type="note">
$n$ can be any positive whole number. Only the commonly drawn Bohr levels stop around 6.
</Callout>

**Example: $2p^5$.** Arrows are electrons and boxes are orbitals:

| $m_l = -1$ | $m_l = 0$ | $m_l = +1$ |
|---|---|---|
| ↑↓ | ↑↓ | ↑ |

Jaya's notes list the last electron as $n = 2$, $l = 1$, $m_l = 0$, $m_s = +\tfrac{1}{2}$.

<Callout type="warning">
**Correction:** using the usual convention (each orbital gets its up arrow, $+\tfrac{1}{2}$, before
any down arrows), the order of filling is ↑ in −1, 0, +1, then ↓ in −1, then ↓ in 0. So the 5th
electron is the **down** arrow in $m_l = 0$: $(2, 1, 0, -\tfrac{1}{2})$.
</Callout>

- Orbitals per subshell $= 2l + 1$
- Max e⁻ per shell $= 2n^2$

## Filling rules

- **Aufbau's principle**: fill lower energy levels first.
- **Pauli's exclusion**: 2 e⁻ per orbital, with opposite spins.
- **Hund's rule**: singly fill equal-energy orbitals before pairing.
