## Ch 6 theory

- The **electronic structure** of an atom describes the energies and arrangement of electrons around the atom.
- Electromagnetic radiation and visible light move through a vacuum at the speed of light.
- **Quantum theory**: energy is quantized and can only have certain allowed values.
- Quantum theory explains the **photoelectric effect**.
- Each energy packet is a **photon**. Each photon carries energy $E = h\nu$.
- Dispersion of radiation into its component wavelengths gives a **spectrum**.
  - Contains all wavelengths = **continuous spectrum**
  - Specific wavelengths only = **line spectrum**
- The **Bohr model** explains the line spectrum of the hydrogen atom.
- The rest is electron configurations and noble-gas configurations.

## Vocabulary

- **Photoelectric effect**: shining light on a metal emits electrons.
- **Emission spectra**: emission of light from electronically excited gas atoms.
- **"Quanta"**: a photon of energy. Energy comes in packets. *(Might use this vocab on the test.)*
- **Uncertainty principle**: you can't know the speed and location of an electron at the same time.

## Ch 6 equations

$$
c = \lambda\nu \qquad E = h\nu \qquad E = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \qquad h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J·s}
$$

**Units:**
- Micrometers: $1 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m} = 1\ \text{µm}$
- Nanometers: $1 \times 10^{-9}\ \text{m} = 1\ \text{nm}$

## Rydberg (Bohr) equations

$$
\Delta E = -2.18 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{J}\left(\frac{1}{n_f^2} - \frac{1}{n_i^2}\right)
$$

$$
E = 2.18 \times 10^{-18}\left(\frac{1}{n^2}\right)
$$

<Callout type="warning">
**Correction:** the energy-level equation needs a minus sign and units:
$E_n = -2.18 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{J}\left(\frac{1}{n^2}\right)$. Every level is negative, which
is why "n gets greater → less negative" below works. On the test, the teacher grades only the
magnitude of a single level's energy, but the sign of $\Delta E$ (emitted vs. absorbed) does count.
</Callout>

## Bohr's model (hydrogen atom)

Sophia's drawing shows the nucleus with two rings, $n = 1$ and $n = 2$:

- An electron on $n = 1$ is in the **ground state**: lowest energy, most stable.
- An electron on $n = 2$ is in an **excited state**.
- As $n$ gets greater, the energy gets **less negative**.
- $n = 2$ to $n = 1$ → **emission**
- $n = 1$ to $n = 2$ → **absorbance** (absorption)

## Filling rules

- **Aufbau principle**: fill in the lowest energy level first.
- **Pauli exclusion principle**: no 2 electrons of a given atom can have the same set of quantum numbers.
- **Hund's rule**: fill in each orbital with an upward arrow first: ↑ ↑ ↑

<Callout type="note">
A more exact version of Hund's rule: put one electron in each equal-energy orbital, all with the
same spin, before pairing any. Drawing the first arrow in each orbital pointing up is the usual
convention.
</Callout>
