The test is **40 multiple choice and 2 free response**. You get a periodic table, but **not** the
constants. This page is the short version of the Chapter 6 and 7 lessons.

## Constants to memorize

| Constant | Value |
|---|---|
| Planck's constant, $h$ | $6.626 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J·s}$ |
| Speed of light, $c$ | $2.998 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s}$ |
| Bohr/Rydberg energy constant | $2.18 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{J}$ |
| Avogadro's number | $6.022 \times 10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$ |
| Units | $1\ \text{nm} = 10^{-9}\ \text{m}$, $1\ \text{µm} = 10^{-6}\ \text{m}$, $1\ \text{Å} = 10^{-10}\ \text{m}$, $1\ \text{MHz} = 10^{6}\ \text{s}^{-1}$ |

## Equations

| Equation | Use it for |
|---|---|
| $c = \lambda\nu$ | wavelength ↔ frequency (λ in **m**) |
| $E = h\nu = hc/\lambda$ | energy of **one** photon |
| $E \times 6.022 \times 10^{23}$ | energy per **mole** of photons |
| $KE = E_{\text{photon}} - E_{\text{minimum}}$ | photoelectric effect |
| $E_n = -2.18 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{J}\,(1/n^2)$ | hydrogen energy level |
| $\Delta E = -2.18 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{J}\,(1/n_f^2 - 1/n_i^2)$ | hydrogen jump; negative = emitted, positive = absorbed |
| $n^2$, $2n^2$ | orbitals, max electrons in shell $n$ |
| $Z_{\text{eff}} = Z - S$ | Slater: same $n$ 0.35, $n - 1$ 0.85, $n - 2$ and lower 1.00 |

## Rules and definitions

- **Quantum numbers**: $n = 1, 2, \ldots$; $l = 0$ to $n - 1$ (s, p, d, f); $m_l = -l$ to $+l$; $m_s = \pm\tfrac{1}{2}$.
- **Aufbau**: fill lowest energy first. **Pauli**: no two electrons share all four quantum numbers, so at most 2 per orbital with opposite spins. **Hund**: one per orbital, same spin, before pairing.
- **Exceptions**: Cr is $[\ce{Ar}]\,4s^1\,3d^5$, Cu is $[\ce{Ar}]\,4s^1\,3d^{10}$.
- **Transition-metal ions** lose 4s before 3d: $\ce{Fe^3+}$ is $[\ce{Ar}]\,3d^5$.
- **Bohr model**: works only for one-electron species. Ground state is $n = 1$. Line spectra happen because energy levels are quantized.
- **Uncertainty principle**: you can't know an electron's exact position and momentum at once, so we use orbitals, not orbits.
- In **hydrogen**, 2s and 2p have the same energy. In **every other atom**, 2s is lower than 2p.
- **Photoelectric effect**: below the threshold, no electrons leave however bright the light. Brighter light means more photons, not stronger ones.

## Trends

- **Radius**: increases down a group and to the left. Cation is smaller than its atom, anion is larger. In an isoelectronic series, more protons means smaller.
- **Ionization energy**: increases up a group and to the right. He is highest. Successive IEs always increase, with a huge jump once the core is reached.
- **IE dips**: group 2 → 13 (p electron easier to remove) and group 15 → 16 (paired-electron repulsion).
- **Electron affinity**: more negative going right. Halogens are most negative; noble gases are positive.
- **Electronegativity**: increases up and to the right. F is highest.
- **Zeff**: rises across a period, roughly constant down a group.

## Mistakes that cost points

1. Forgetting to convert nm to m.
2. Using a negative $\Delta E$ in $E = hc/\lambda$. Use the size; wavelengths are positive.
3. Mixing up $n^2$ (orbitals) and $2n^2$ (electrons).
4. Counting unpaired electrons from the superscript instead of the orbital diagram.
5. Removing 3d electrons before 4s when making a transition-metal ion.
6. Counting the electron itself in Slater's rules.
7. Explaining a trend with "it's farther right" instead of protons, shielding, Zeff, or distance.
8. FRQs: show the formula, plug in with units, give the answer with units and sig figs, then explain in a sentence.
