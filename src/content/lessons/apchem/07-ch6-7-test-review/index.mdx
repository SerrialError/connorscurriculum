### Theory checklist

Be able to explain each of these in a sentence or two:

- Why the photoelectric effect showed light comes in packets, and why brighter light can't make up for low frequency.
- Why a line spectrum is evidence that electron energies are quantized.
- How wavelength, frequency, and photon energy compare when the numbers are given. You **don't** need
  to memorize the order of the spectrum regions (gamma, X-ray, UV, and so on) for this test.
- What Bohr got right and what he got wrong.
- What the uncertainty principle means, and why it leads to orbitals instead of orbits.
- That all moving matter has wave properties, which only matter for tiny particles like electrons.
- What each quantum number describes, and the rules for which values are allowed.
- Why 2s is lower than 2p in many-electron atoms (shielding and penetration), and why 4s fills before 3d.
- The reasoning behind the aufbau principle, the Pauli principle, and Hund's rule.
- What Zeff is, and how it and distance explain **every** trend: radius, ion size, IE, EA, electronegativity, and metallic character.
- How Mendeleev organized the table, and why atomic number replaced atomic mass.
- How metals, nonmetals, and metalloids differ, including the acid/base behavior of their oxides.
- Group trends: alkali and alkaline earth metals, hydrogen, oxygen, the halogens, and the noble gases. See [Groups and Families](../07-groups-and-families/).

## The equation sheet

The test comes with a periodic table and the official
[AP Chemistry equation sheet](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-chemistry-equations-sheet.pdf).
You don't need to memorize anything that is on it.

**Given on the sheet:**

- Planck's constant, $h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J·s}$, and the speed of light, $c = 2.998 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s}$
- Avogadro's number, $6.022 \times 10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$
- $E = h\nu$, $c = \lambda\nu$, and $1\ \text{Hz} = 1\ \text{s}^{-1}$
- Metric prefixes: giga $10^{9}$, mega $10^{6}$, kilo $10^{3}$, centi $10^{-2}$, milli $10^{-3}$, micro $10^{-6}$, nano $10^{-9}$, pico $10^{-12}$
- Coulomb's law, $F \propto q_1 q_2 / r^2$, which is the reason behind every Chapter 7 trend

**Not on the sheet, so memorize:**

- The Bohr energy constant, $2.18 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{J}$, and the $E_n$ and $\Delta E$ equations below
- $1\ \text{Å} = 10^{-10}\ \text{m}$
- de Broglie's $\lambda = h/mv$
- $KE = E_{\text{photon}} - E_{\text{minimum}}$ for the photoelectric effect
- $n^2$ orbitals and $2n^2$ electrons in shell $n$, and the quantum number rules
- Slater's rules (0.35, 0.85, 1.00)

## Equations

| Equation | Use it for |
|---|---|
| $c = \lambda\nu$ | wavelength ↔ frequency (λ in **m**) |
| $E = h\nu = hc/\lambda$ | energy of **one** photon |
| $E \times 6.022 \times 10^{23}$ | energy per **mole** of photons |
| $KE = E_{\text{photon}} - E_{\text{minimum}}$ | photoelectric effect |
| $E_n = -2.18 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{J}\,(1/n^2)$ | hydrogen energy level (the teacher grades the size; the sign doesn't matter for one level) |
| $\Delta E = -2.18 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{J}\,(1/n_f^2 - 1/n_i^2)$ | hydrogen jump; negative = emitted, positive = absorbed |
| $n^2$, $2n^2$ | orbitals, max electrons in shell $n$ |
| $Z_{\text{eff}} = Z - S$ | Slater: same $n$ 0.35, $n - 1$ 0.85, $n - 2$ and lower 1.00 |

## Rules and definitions

- **Quantum numbers**: $n = 1, 2, \ldots$; $l = 0$ to $n - 1$ (s, p, d, f); $m_l = -l$ to $+l$; $m_s = \pm\tfrac{1}{2}$.
- **Aufbau**: fill lowest energy first. **Pauli**: no two electrons share all four quantum numbers, so at most 2 per orbital with opposite spins. **Hund**: one per orbital, same spin, before pairing.
- **Exceptions**: Cr is $[\ce{Ar}]\,4s^1\,3d^5$, Cu is $[\ce{Ar}]\,4s^1\,3d^{10}$.
- **Transition-metal ions** lose 4s before 3d: $\ce{Fe^3+}$ is $[\ce{Ar}]\,3d^5$.
- **Bohr model**: works only for one-electron species. Ground state is $n = 1$. Line spectra happen because energy levels are quantized.
- **Uncertainty principle**: you can't know an electron's exact position and momentum at once, so we use orbitals, not orbits.
- In **hydrogen**, 2s and 2p have the same energy. In **every other atom**, 2s is lower than 2p.
- **Photoelectric effect**: below the threshold, no electrons leave however bright the light. Brighter light means more photons, not stronger ones.

## Trends

- **Radius**: increases down a group and to the left. Cation is smaller than its atom, anion is larger. In an isoelectronic series, more protons means smaller.
- **Ionization energy**: increases up a group and to the right. He is highest. Successive IEs always increase, with a huge jump once the core is reached.
- **IE dips**: group 2 → 13 (p electron easier to remove) and group 15 → 16 (paired-electron repulsion).
- **Electron affinity**: more negative going right. Halogens are most negative; noble gases are positive.
- **Electronegativity**: increases up and to the right. F is highest.
- **Zeff**: rises across a period, roughly constant down a group.

## Common mistakes

1. Forgetting to convert nm to m.
2. Using a negative $\Delta E$ in $E = hc/\lambda$. Use the size; wavelengths are positive.
   (For a single level, $E_n$, only the size is graded. For $\Delta E$, the sign tells emitted vs. absorbed, so keep it.)
3. Mixing up $n^2$ (orbitals) and $2n^2$ (electrons).
4. Counting unpaired electrons from the superscript instead of the orbital diagram.
5. Removing 3d electrons before 4s when making a transition-metal ion.
6. Counting the electron itself in Slater's rules.
7. Explaining a trend with "it's farther right" instead of protons, shielding, Zeff, or distance.
8. FRQs: show the formula, plug in with units, give the answer with units and sig figs, then explain in a sentence.
