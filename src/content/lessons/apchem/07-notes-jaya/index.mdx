## Formula

$$
Z_{\text{eff}} = Z - S
$$

- $Z$ = protons in the nucleus
- $S$ = inner-shell electrons

<Callout type="note">
Counting $S$ as the number of inner-shell electrons is the quick estimate. Slater's rules give a more
exact $S$: 0.35 for each other electron in the same shell, 0.85 for each in shell $n - 1$, and 1.00
for each in $n - 2$ and lower.
</Callout>

## Trends

**IE (ionization energy)**: the energy required to remove an electron from a gaseous atom.
Jaya wrote the equation as $\ce{X-(g)} + 0 \rightarrow \ce{X(g)} + e$.

<Callout type="warning">
**Correction:** the first ionization energy equation is $\ce{X(g) -> X+(g) + e-}$. The atom starts
neutral and ends up as a **positive** ion.
</Callout>

**EA (electron affinity)**: the energy required to gain an electron.

<Callout type="warning">
**Correction:** for most atoms, energy is **released** when an electron is added, not required.
Electron affinity is the energy **change** for $\ce{X(g) + e- -> X-(g)}$, and it's usually negative.
</Callout>

## Periodic table trends

Jaya's diagram of the periodic table:

| Direction | What happens |
|---|---|
| Toward the top right | **high nuclear charge** |
| Diagonally toward the bottom left | decreasing nuclear charge |
| Right to left (←) | **decreasing** ionization energy, **increasing** atomic radius |
| Top to bottom (↓) | **decreasing** ionization energy, **increasing** atomic radius |

**The smaller an atom is, the harder it is to remove an electron!**

<Callout type="note">
"Nuclear charge" in the diagram means **effective** nuclear charge on the valence electrons. The
actual number of protons goes up across *and* down, but Zeff rises across a period and changes little
down a group.
</Callout>
