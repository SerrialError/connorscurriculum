## Effective nuclear charge

$$
Z_{\text{eff}} = Z - S
$$

- $Z$ = nuclear charge
- $S$ = shielding constant

**Simple rule: JUST LOOK AT VALENCE e⁻!!!** No need for a calculation. It is easier than you think.

## Slater's rules: finding Zeff for K

**Step 1: find the configuration** and group it by shell:

| $1s^2$ | $2s^2\,2p^6$ | $3s^2\,3p^6$ | $4s^1$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $n - 3$ | $n - 2$ | $n - 1$ | $n$ |

**Step 2: get rid of the last guy.** In this case $4s^1$ is kicked out, because it's the electron
we're finding Zeff for.

**Step 3: multiply each group by its shielding value and subtract from Z:**

$$
19 - \big[(2 \times 1) + (8 \times 1) + (8 \times 0.85)\big]
$$

Sophia's notes give the answer as $Z_{\text{eff}}(\ce{K}) = 2.02$.

<Callout type="warning">
**Correction:** the setup is right, but the arithmetic is off.
$(2 \times 1) + (8 \times 1) + (8 \times 0.85) = 2 + 8 + 6.80 = 16.80$, so
$Z_{\text{eff}} = 19 - 16.80 = \mathbf{2.20}$.
</Callout>

## Trends

- **Effective nuclear charge** increases from left to right across a period.
- **Atomic radius** increases going diagonally to the left (down and to the left).
  - It increases from top to bottom.

## Ionization energy

$$
\ce{X(g) -> X+(g) + e-}
$$

**2nd:**

$$
\ce{X+(g) -> X^2+(g) + e-}
$$

And so on with the 3rd, 4th, and so on. The atom is **losing** an electron.

## Electron affinity

The atom **gains** an electron:

$$
\ce{X(g) + e- -> X-(g)}
$$
