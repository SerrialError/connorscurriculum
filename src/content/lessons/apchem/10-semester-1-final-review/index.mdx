## What to memorize

The final comes with a periodic table and the
[AP Chemistry equation sheet](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-chemistry-equations-sheet.pdf).
These equations from the semester aren't on it:

| Equation | Use it for |
|---|---|
| $\dfrac{(\text{atoms of element})(\text{atomic weight})}{\text{formula weight}} \times 100\%$ | percent composition |
| $\dfrac{\text{actual}}{\text{theoretical}} \times 100\%$ | percent yield |
| $M_1V_1 = M_2V_2$ | dilution |
| $\Delta E = q + w$ | first law: q > 0 for heat into the system, w > 0 for work done **on** it |
| $q_\text{rxn} = -q_\text{soln}$ | calorimetry |

| $2n^2$ | electrons in shell $n$ ($n^2$ orbitals) |

| $\tfrac{1}{2}(\text{bonding} - \text{antibonding electrons})$ | bond order from a molecular orbital diagram |

### Chapters 1–3

- **Significant figures:** nonzero digits, zeros between them, and trailing zeros after a decimal point count; leading zeros never do. ×, ÷: fewest significant figures. +, −: fewest decimal places. Counted and defined numbers are exact.
- **Accuracy** is closeness to the true value; **precision** is closeness of repeated measurements to each other.
- **Matter:** elements, compounds, and homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures; physical vs. chemical changes and properties; intensive vs. extensive properties.
- **Laws:** conservation of mass, definite proportions (a compound always has the same composition), and multiple proportions (whole-number mass ratios between two compounds of the same elements).
- **Experiments:** Thomson's cathode rays (electrons, charge-to-mass ratio), Millikan's oil drops (the electron's charge), Rutherford's gold foil (a small, dense, positive nucleus).
- **Ions and names:** common ion charges by group, the 16 polyatomic ions plus hydronium, Roman numerals for metals with more than one charge, prefixes for molecular compounds, and acid names (‑ate → ‑ic acid, ‑ite → ‑ous acid).
- **Limiting reactant:** the one that makes the least product, found with mole ratios, not grams.

### Chapters 4–5

- **Strong acids:** HCl, HBr, HI, $\ce{HNO3}$, $\ce{HClO3}$, $\ce{HClO4}$, $\ce{H2SO4}$. **Strong bases:** group 1 hydroxides and $\ce{Ca(OH)2}$, $\ce{Sr(OH)2}$, $\ce{Ba(OH)2}$.
- **Acid and base definitions:** Arrhenius (makes $\ce{H+}$ or $\ce{OH-}$ in water) and Brønsted–Lowry (proton donor or acceptor).
- **Electrolytes:** strong (soluble ionic compounds, strong acids and bases), weak (weak acids and bases), and nonelectrolytes (most molecular compounds).
- **Solubility:** compounds of $\ce{Na+}$, $\ce{K+}$, $\ce{NH4+}$, and $\ce{NO3-}$ always dissolve, plus the longer [solubility guidelines](../04-precipitation-and-net-ionic-equations/) from class.
- **Oxidation numbers** and the rules for assigning them. Oxidation is losing electrons, reduction is gaining them. The species reduced is the oxidizing agent.
- **Balancing redox in acid:** balance atoms other than O and H, then O with $\ce{H2O}$, H with $\ce{H+}$, and charge with electrons; multiply so the electrons cancel, then add.
- **Signs:** exothermic is ΔH < 0 (surroundings warm up). ΔH scales with the coefficients and flips sign when the equation is reversed. Elements in their standard states have ΔH°f = 0.

### Chapters 6–7

- **Quantum numbers:** $l$ from 0 to $n - 1$, $m_l$ from $-l$ to $+l$, $m_s = \pm\tfrac{1}{2}$.
- **Aufbau** (lowest energy first), **Pauli** (two electrons per orbital, opposite spins), **Hund** (one per orbital before pairing). Cr and Cu exceptions. Transition metals lose 4s before 3d.
- **Bohr model:** quantized levels explain line spectra. Its limitation: it works only for one-electron species.
- **Trends:** radius increases down and to the left; ionization energy, electron affinity (more negative), and electronegativity increase up and to the right. Cations are smaller than their atoms, anions larger. Explain every trend with Zeff and distance from the nucleus.

### Chapters 8–9

- **Bonds:** ionic (metal + nonmetal), covalent (nonmetals; polar if their electronegativities differ), metallic (cations in a sea of electrons).
- **Lattice energy** grows with larger ion charges and smaller ions. It was left off the Chapters 8–9 test, but it's on the final's topic list.
- **Lewis structures:** best structure has formal charges closest to zero, with negative charges on the more electronegative atoms. Resonance structures differ only in where electrons are.
- **Bond order, length, and strength:** more bonds between the same two atoms means shorter and stronger.
- **Shapes and hybridization:** the shape chart and hybridization cards on the [Chapters 8–9 review sheet](../09-ch8-9-test-review/#shapes). A single bond is 1 σ, a double is 1 σ + 1 π, a triple is 1 σ + 2 π.
- **Molecular orbitals:** for B₂, C₂, and N₂, π₂ₚ is below σ₂ₚ; for O₂ and F₂, σ₂ₚ is below π₂ₚ. Unpaired electrons make a molecule paramagnetic. Bond order 0 means the molecule doesn't form.

### Chapter 10

- **Gas laws** come from $PV = nRT$ and $\dfrac{P_1V_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{P_2V_2}{T_2}$ (both on the sheet): Boyle ($P$ and $V$ inverse), Charles ($V$ and $T$ direct), Avogadro ($V$ and $n$ direct). Temperatures always in kelvins.
- **Gas stoichiometry:** use $PV = nRT$ to turn a gas's pressure and volume into moles, then use the mole ratio as in Chapter 3. Molar mass is $\mathcal{M} = DRT/P$.
- **Dalton's law:** total pressure is the sum of the partial pressures, and each gas's share is its mole fraction. For a gas collected over water, subtract the water's vapor pressure.
- **Kinetic-molecular theory:** gas particles are in continuous random motion, their own volume is negligible, they neither attract nor repel each other, and their collisions are elastic. Average kinetic energy is proportional to Kelvin temperature, so at the same temperature, lighter particles move faster.
- **Effusion and diffusion:** lighter gases effuse and diffuse faster (Graham's law).
- **Real gases** deviate from ideal behavior at **high pressure** (particle volume matters) and **low temperature** (attractions matter). Small, nonpolar particles with few electrons, such as He and $\ce{H2}$, behave most nearly ideally.

## Common mistakes

1. Using mL instead of L in molarity, or adding the full final volume of water when diluting.
2. Comparing grams instead of moles to find the limiting reactant.
3. Forgetting to divide ΔH by the coefficient when the equation is written for more than one mole.
4. Doing products minus reactants with bond enthalpies (it's broken minus formed).
5. Converting a temperature **change** from kelvins to Celsius. A change of 20 K is a change of 20 °C.
6. Using Celsius in a gas law.
7. Forgetting to convert nm to m in $c = \lambda\nu$.
8. Mixing up $n^2$ (orbitals) and $2n^2$ (electrons).
9. Explaining a trend with "it's farther right" instead of Zeff and distance.
10. Using the O₂ orbital order for B₂, C₂, or N₂.
11. FRQs: show the setup with units, give the answer with units and significant figures, and explain in a sentence.
